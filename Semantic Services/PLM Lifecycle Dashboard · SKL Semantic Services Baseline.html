SKL Semantic Services Baseline

Set up and operationalize the services that bring in customer terminology, process it into scoped vocabulary extensions, and publish the SKL’s definitions downstream. This is the baseline every later project builds on, and it will evolve to support configuration, customization, personalization, provisioning and insulation at scale. Visier is the first target.

      Design decision: keep alternate labels out of the core. Insperity and customer labels live in scoped lexicons that point at core concepts by ID. New customer-only concepts live in customer extension vocabularies with match relations to the core. The core and domain ontologies stay small, stable and fast, and onboarding a customer adds no triples to them. The decision is made on measured evidence in W2-02 and recorded in W1-05, by G0.

      How the pieces fit

      Arrows show references. A label record names a concept by ID and never copies it; nothing in the lexicon or extension graphs is imported into the core.

      Triage: where each extracted term goes

      The processing service proposes a class and a destination with evidence; a person decides.

      What a label record carries

      Labels are records in their own right, so each can be scoped, dated, reviewed and retired without touching the concept it names.

      Resolution order

      1. The caller passes a tenant context with the text to resolve.
      2. The service looks in that customer’s lexicon, then the Insperity lexicon, then the domain lexicon, then the core preferred labels.
      3. Other tenants’ lexicons are never searched. Hidden labels match but are never shown.
      4. More than one concept at the same level returns the candidates with their definitions for disambiguation.

      Review and data-entry screens for operations

      Built in W5. Ops reviewers work only in the lexicon and extension graphs; anything that needs a core change escalates to a steward.

      Business value weights

      Risk by workstream

      Likelihood averages five factors scored 1–5: novelty to the organization, technical complexity, readiness gap (inputs, access and baselines missing), dependency depth (workstreams that must land first) and schedule pressure. Impact scales the value of the baseline outcomes that depend on the workstream against the most it could carry. All factor scores are proposals for the steering group to confirm at G0.

      Which outcomes depend on which workstreams

      3 = critical (the outcome fails without it), 2 = supporting, 1 = sustains or measures it. Value at stake = driver weight × stake (1–3, a proposal).
