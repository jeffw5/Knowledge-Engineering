Decision Question Baseline

The questions each persona must answer to make the decisions in their sales journey, baselined across data, content, knowledge, mappings, transformations and advisor responses, so every upstream change can be traced to the decisions it puts at risk. Each agent that evaluates options carries an AI circuit breaker measured by MTBH.

Each job step has one decision and four questions that must be answered well to make it. Tier H questions change the decision or create legal or financial exposure if answered wrongly; they get stricter grounding and breaker thresholds. Golden answers are authored by subject-matter experts and frozen as the baseline at gate G1.

Quality by design treats each decision question as the unit of control. Its baseline record pins everything the answer depends on in six layers and the answer itself. When any pinned item changes, the question is re-run and the new answer is compared with the baseline. The difference is classified, scored against the question’s tier, and routed.

How a change is classified

      

        
Benign The answer is equivalent: same claims, only wording or citation versions moved. Re-baseline automatically.

        
Material A claim changed and the change is explained by a Released upstream change (new rates, new rule). The answer is right but different: an SME confirms and re-baselines.

        
Breaking A claim changed with no upstream change that explains it, a golden claim is missing, a contradiction appears, or grounding falls below the floor. This is a regression or a hallucination: the brief is withdrawn and the agents in its lineage are tested by their breakers.

Critical quality attributes

    
The design space for each layer: what is measured, the acceptance criterion, what the baseline captures, and the signal that triggers a re-run.

    

  







  
Every agent makes decisions: what to classify, map, keep, drop, rank or recommend. Its AI circuit breaker watches the integrity of those decisions with nine signals and measures MTBH, the mean number of outputs between integrity faults (unsupported claims, contradictions, rule violations, baseline regressions). Breakers fail closed: when one opens, the agent stops and a deterministic fallback takes over until a named owner resets it.
