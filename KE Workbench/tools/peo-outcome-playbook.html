Terms you'll use

Desired outcome
A measurable success metric a client uses to judge a job, written as direction + metric + object + clarifier. Example: "Minimize the likelihood of miscalculating overtime under federal and state rules".
Importance and satisfaction
Share of respondents rating the outcome 4 or 5 out of 5, divided by 10. A satisfaction of 5.2 means 52% are satisfied.
Opportunity
Importance + max(Importance − Satisfaction, 0) on a 0–20 scale. Above 15 is extreme, 12–15 high, 10–12 solid.
Significant gap
A PEO vs non-PEO difference larger than its 95% interval. Only significant gaps become claims; smaller ones are directional.
Theme
The service line that owns the outcome (Payroll, Benefits, HR Compliance and so on). Themes tell you who acts on a finding.
Approved use
Where compliance has cleared an outcome to appear: the survey, internal sales guidance and the AI agent, or external marketing claims.

Battle cards for marketing

A battle card speaks to one persona about one theme in the persona's own success measures. Prospects are employers without a PEO, so their needs come from the non-PEO scores; your proof comes from where PEO clients are measurably better served.

  1. Pick the persona and themeStart with the persona who signs or influences the deal and a theme where they are under-served.
  2. Read the unmet needsThe top outcomes by non-PEO opportunity are what this buyer struggles with today. Lead with these, in their words.
  3. Use only defensible proofProof points appear only where PEO clients are significantly better served. Each cites its outcome ID and survey wave.
  4. Prepare honest answersWhere PEO clients are behind, the card says so. Acknowledge it and say what is being done; never claim an advantage there.
  5. Ask, don't assertDiscovery questions turn each outcome into something the prospect answers, so they describe the gap themselves.
  6. Send it through reviewBusiness and compliance SMEs approve the card's outcomes in the review tool. Only outcomes approved for external marketing may appear outside the company.
  7. Refresh every waveRebuild cards when new survey results land. Proof that stops being significant comes off the card.

Continuous improvement and innovation

Each outcome's position for PEO clients and for non-PEO employers says what kind of work it needs. Because every outcome traces to a service, each finding lands with an owner.

FixPEO clients are under-served (opportunity ≥ 12) and significantly worse off than non-PEO employers. Goes to the service line's improvement backlog.
InnovateBoth groups are under-served. Nobody solves it well, so it is a candidate for a new capability or offering.
Protect & marketNon-PEO employers are under-served and PEO clients are not. This is an advantage: keep it and feed it to battle cards.
SimplifyPEO clients are over-served or rate it unimportant. Look for effort or cost to take out.
  1. Find your theme's rowEach service line owns a row. Click a cell to list its outcomes and the services responsible.
  2. Work Fix items firstThey are gaps clients feel and competitors can exploit. Size each with the service and process owners.
  3. Give each outcome an operational measureMost outcomes are already measurable from system data (see the table below). Track it monthly between waves.
  4. Score new conceptsUse the concept scorer: pick the outcomes a concept would improve and compare concepts by the opportunity they close.
  5. Re-measureThe next survey wave, or a pulse on the top outcomes, shows whether the opportunity score moved.

AI sales advisory agent

The agent sits beside a salesperson before or during a conversation. It never answers from general knowledge: it looks up the outcomes, scores and services in the benchmark and builds its advice from them, with citations.

Profile the buyerPersona, company size, industry, states and current HR model, from the CRM and the conversation.
Find what mattersMatch what the prospect says to outcomes for that persona, ranked by non-PEO opportunity.
Advise the repNext discovery question, the proof point that fits (only significant gaps), honest answers and which specialist to involve.
Log and learnOutcomes discussed and the deal result are logged by outcome ID for the next survey wave and the improvement backlog.

The same rules apply to every card, backlog item and agent answer, so marketing, product and sales never contradict each other or the data.
