Customer 360 · qualitative analysis · appreciative inquiry (4-D)

Persona interviews

Interview people who fit a persona about their work at its best, then code what they say into functional, emotional and social jobs, the steps of those jobs and the outcomes they use to judge success. Codes become evidence for the persona model in Buyer Personas & Jobs and candidates for the next Job Study wave.

Method

Appreciative inquiry (Cooperrider and Whitney) asks people about the work when it goes well, rather than starting from problems. The 4-D cycle structures the conversation: Discover peak experiences and what made them possible, Dream what the work would be like if it always went that way, Design the steps and conditions that would make that real, and Destiny (deliver) what must be true, and how they would measure it, to sustain it.

Jobs to be done (Clayton Christensen) treats a customer as hiring a product to make progress in a circumstance. Every job has a functional dimension, what they are trying to get done, and emotional and social dimensions, how they want to feel and be seen. Peak-experience stories surface all three naturally.

Job maps and desired outcomes (Outcome-Driven Innovation) break a functional job into the universal steps Define, Locate, Prepare, Confirm, Execute, Monitor, Modify and Conclude, and state each success measure as direction + metric + object of control + clarifier, for example Minimize the time required to confirm that pay calculations are correct before funds are released. That format is what the Job Study survey rates.

How many interviews. Two or three per persona (15–20 in all) before fielding the survey, as the benchmark design calls for. Stop adding interviews for a persona when new ones stop producing new codes.

Coding rules

  1. Code the participant's words, not your summary. Select the passage in the notes and pick what it evidences.
  2. If the passage supports something already in the model, link it as evidence for an existing job, step or outcome. Only create a new candidate when nothing existing fits.
  3. State jobs without naming a solution: Pay employees accurately and on time, not Use the payroll portal.
  4. Write outcomes with the builder so they share the survey format; place each on the universal step where the executor judges it.
  5. Emotional jobs start with Feel or Avoid feeling; social jobs with Be seen as or Be perceived as.
  6. Never record a participant's name or anything that identifies them or their employer. Record consent before publishing.
  7. Spell out acronyms in statements where you can; preferred labels must not contain them, so unknown ones are flagged.
