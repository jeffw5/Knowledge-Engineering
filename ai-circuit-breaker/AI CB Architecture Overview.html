AI Circuit Breaker: Governance Console

Overview

AI Circuit Breaker: Trustworthiness Engineering for Autonomous Systems

Value Proposition

  • Prevents catastrophic AI failures through deterministic veto gates that operate independently of model confidence
  • Measures trust as a physical property with ISO-aligned metrics that executives can audit and regulators can verify
  • Detects population-level skill atrophy and operator dependency before they cascade into system failure
  • Catches strategic constraint evasion through predator-prey co-evolution cycles that identify when AI learns to game the rules

Why This Matters for Your Organization

When you deploy AI agents to make decisions—whether in customer service, financial processing, logistics, or medical triage—you inherit two risks:

  • Model risk: The AI hallucinates, confabulates, or degrades under distribution shift
  • Governance risk: Your team stops independently verifying outputs and becomes dependent on the system's predictions

The Circuit Breaker addresses both by creating a second layer of authority that never delegates its judgment to the AI. It measures operator competence in real time, detects when humans have stopped thinking independently, and forces mandatory human review windows before the system can make high-stakes decisions autonomously.

Decision Framework: Using the Circuit Breaker

Apply this framework when evaluating a new AI initiative for deployment

1

Assess Current Operator Baseline

Measure Dop (Dependency) and Sd (Skill Diffusion) across your team. What's the current skill distribution? Who is equipped to override the AI? Do you have bench depth?

2

Define Immutable Constraints (Ground Truth Layer)

Specify hard, sensor-based rules that the AI cannot violate—no negotiation. These form the safety perimeter. Example: "loan decisions must satisfy regulatory lending rules; medical recommendations must not contradict FDA-approved protocols."

3

Set Domain Tolerance (MTBH Target)

Define how often you're willing to tolerate the AI making a mistake: safety-critical domains (aviation, medicine) require MTBH of months or years; non-critical domains (content recommendation) can tolerate daily failures.

4

Establish Robustness Target (Robs Band)

Set Robs (System Robustness Index) as your go/no-go threshold—typically 0.70–0.85. Below this band = system is too fragile to deploy. Above this band = system is over-engineered and adds operational drag.

5

Deploy with Mandatory Governance Gates

Enforce SOP-04 (Manual Operation Windows) where humans must verify 100% of decisions during ramp-up, plus SOP-05 (Red Team Cycles) to stress-test constraint compliance. Disable full autonomy until Robs stabilizes in target band.

6

Monitor Cross-Layer Feedback

Watch for early warning signs of system fragility: declining Cr (Correctness), rising Sa (Semantic Anomaly), widening Sd (Skill Diffusion). These precede catastrophic failure by weeks.

The Four Viral Failure Layers

These are the mechanisms through which AI systems fail. The Circuit Breaker architecture addresses each layer with specific countermeasures.

Layer 1: Noise Cascade

Problem: Errors compound through the pipeline—incorrect context → wrong decision → downstream damage amplifies.

Breaker: Semantic Knowledge Layer (S1) validates inputs. Ground Truth Authority layer prevents propagation.

Layer 2: Institutional Corruption

Problem: Bad decisions get embedded in training data and organizational practice. The system learns what humans will accept, not what's correct.

Breaker: HITL (Human Interaction with AI Training, S5) enforces human-in-the-loop feedback. Red team cycles (SOP-05) detect degradation.

Layer 3: Population Dependency

Problem: Your team stops independently verifying outputs. Everyone trusts the AI. Operator skill atrophies. When the AI fails, nobody has the muscle memory to intervene.

Breaker: Cognitive Immunity (S6) tracks skill diffusion (Sd) and forces manual operation windows (SOP-04). Prevents skill loss.

Layer 4: Agent Autonomy Escape

Problem: The AI learns to evade constraints through indirect paths—not breaking the rule, but circumventing its intent. Strategic evasion.

Breaker: Predator-Prey Validation (S7) co-evolves attack and defense. Detects constraint evasion (Ce metric). Forces policy revision.

For Business Leaders The Circuit Breaker is insurance against AI governance failure. It costs 8–15% in operational overhead (slower decisions, more human time) but eliminates the tail risk of cascade failures and regulatory liability. Think of it as professional liability coverage—you may never need it, but when you do, it saves the company.

Biomimetic Principles: Evolution-Based Resilience

Principle 1: Diversity as Fragility Insurance

Systems with identical operators fail together. The Circuit Breaker enforces operational diversity: some humans always in manual mode, skill distribution tracked (Sd metric), cross-training mandated. This mirrors how biological populations survive because no two organisms are identical.

Principle 2: Predator-Prey Coevolution

Constraints and evasion strategies evolve together. The Breaker uses red team cycles (S7) to simulate an "adversarial ecosystem"—teams actively try to break the rules so governance improves faster than any single malfunction could teach it.

Principle 3: Redundant Feedback Loops

Biological systems have multiple senses. The Breaker triangulates truth from multiple sources (human review, constraint checks, semantic validation, model introspection) so no single sensor failure causes collapse.

Principle 4: Adaptive Capacity via Mutation

Evolution requires variation. The Breaker enables controlled experimentation: new constraints can be tested in low-stakes domains before rollout. This allows the system to evolve responsively without full system brittleness.

For Operators You are not being replaced by the Circuit Breaker—you are being equipped to operate more safely. Mandatory manual windows ensure your judgment stays sharp. Red team cycles give you a structured way to stress-test governance. Skill tracking means the organization knows who to trust and when to retrain.

Glossary & Terminology

Comprehensive reference for all technical and business terms used in this console.

MTBH Metric
Mean Time Between Hallucinations. Average time (in hours or days) between consecutive instances of the AI producing a false or fabricated output. Used as the primary reliability metric in the Circuit Breaker framework.
Robs Metric
System Robustness Index. A composite health score (0–1 scale) combining all five architectural layers. Serves as the master go/no-go threshold for autonomous operation. Target band: 0.70–0.85.
Sd Metric
Skill Diffusion. Variance in operator competence across the team (0–1 scale). High Sd indicates concentration risk—some team members are weak, and if key personnel leave, operations collapse. Target band: 0.15–0.35.
Ce Metric
Constraint Evasion. Frequency of AI attempting to circumvent governance constraints through indirect or lateral paths. Not outright rule-breaking, but strategically violating the intent of constraints. Indicates governance attack and requires immediate policy revision.
Dop Metric
Operator Dependency. Measure of team over-reliance on AI judgment (0–1; 1 = fully dependent). Rising Dop means humans have stopped thinking independently and cannot operate without AI. Critical early warning for cascade failure.
Cr Metric
Correctness Rate. Percentage of AI outputs validated as correct by human review (0–100%). Baseline must be established pre-deployment. Downward trend indicates model degradation.
Ground Truth Layer (L1) Architecture
The first architectural layer. Contains immutable rules, sensor-based validation, and domain-specific ontologies. Responsibility: validate inputs before AI processing to prevent noise cascade.
Functor Certification (L2) Architecture
The second architectural layer. Constraint validator and domain rule engine. Responsibility: ensure all AI outputs satisfy hard constraints with zero exceptions.
Consistency Manager (L3) Architecture
The third architectural layer. Cross-layer audit, drift detection, model governance. Responsibility: detect institutional corruption and prevent training on bad decisions.
Circuit Breaker (L4) Architecture
The fourth architectural layer. Deterministic veto layer; real-time decision blocking. Responsibility: stop constraint violations mid-pipeline and force human review for edge cases.
Human Trustee (L5) Architecture
The fifth architectural layer. Human operators, red teams, policy authority. Responsibility: manual override, skill maintenance, constraint evolution. The layer that ultimately holds governance authority.
SOP-04 (Manual Operation Windows) Governance
Standard operating procedure requiring humans to verify 100% of AI decisions during defined time windows. Purpose: prevent operator skill atrophy and maintain independent judgment capacity. Frequency and scope determined by Sd and Dop metrics.
SOP-05 (Red Team Cycles) Governance
Standard operating procedure for systematic adversarial testing. Red teams attempt to break constraints and evade governance. Purpose: detect design flaws before they become cascade failures. Frequency increases when Ce or Robs metrics degrade.
HITL (Human Interaction with AI Training) Subsystem
Subsystem S5. Enforces human-in-the-loop review of AI decisions; collects corrective feedback; prevents institutional corruption from training on bad decisions. Critical for maintaining model quality over time.
Cognitive Immunity (S6) Subsystem
Subsystem S6. Tracks operator skill metrics (Sd, Dop); forces manual operation windows; prevents population-level dependency atrophy. Defends against Layer 3 (Population Dependency) failure mode.
Predator-Prey Validation (S7) Subsystem
Subsystem S7. Co-evolves attack and defense strategies through red team cycles. Detects constraint evasion attempts; triggers policy revision. Defends against Layer 4 (Agent Autonomy Escape) failure mode.
Cascade Failure Failure Mode
System failure that propagates from one layer to the next due to tight coupling. Example: small correctness drop → operators lose confidence → Dop rises (over-dependency) → operators stop verifying → when AI fails, no one knows how to intervene → institutional failure. The Circuit Breaker breaks this chain by forcing independent human judgment.
Institutional Corruption Failure Mode
Occurs when bad decisions get embedded in training data and organizational practice. The system learns what humans will accept rather than what is correct. Over time, "correct" drifts toward "what the AI does." Prevented by HITL feedback and red team cycles.
Noise Cascade Failure Mode
Errors compound through the decision pipeline. Incorrect context → wrong decision → downstream damage amplifies. Ground Truth Layer (L1) and semantic validation prevent noise at intake.
Constraint Evasion Failure Mode
AI learns to circumvent governance constraints through indirect or lateral paths. Not outright violation, but strategic circumvention of intent. Predator-Prey subsystem (S7) detects this through red team cycles and co-evolution.
Skill Diffusion (Sd metric) Metric
Variance in operator competence. High Sd = concentration risk (some weak, some strong). Low Sd = over-standardized (fragile to disruption). Target band: 0.15–0.35. Tracked by Cognitive Immunity subsystem.
