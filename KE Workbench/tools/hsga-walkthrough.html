A thin layer of meaning on top of the systems you already run

Nothing gets ripped out. The architecture adds a governed semantic scaffold between your legacy systems and vendor tools below and the people, dashboards and AI agents above. Rules are written once, compiled into each tool's own language, and checked at every layer boundary.

What "holonic" means here

Koestler's term for something that is a self-governing whole and a part of a larger whole at the same time. Three properties must hold at every level.

The baseline

Three semantic services carry everything

Inbound brings content in, processing turns it into governed meaning, outbound delivers it. Review screens, provisioning, monitoring, triage and simulation are operational extensions built on the services' contracts. None of them touch a graph.

Scaffold over legacy

Three tiers: the scaffold sits on top, legacy stays as it is

The enterprise semantic stack has three tiers, not two. Top-down ontology and bottom-up implementation meet at a persistent mediation holon. A legacy change never reaches the ontology, and a rule is never re-authored inside a legacy tool.

Three tiers, not two

Top-down meaning and bottom-up implementation change at different speeds, under different owners. A dedicated middle tier sits between them so neither has to absorb the other's rate of change.
